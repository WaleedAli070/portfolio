# Ideas & Notes

## Engg Management

### Part 1: (Oct, 2024)

- Being the new EM + Software Architect + Developer + DevOps at the same time, just saw through a major migration/release. Chaotic Days but great learning. Management struggles: It's been just over a year now since I made the transition from an Individual Contributor to an Engineering Manager, and it's been a rollercoaster ride, to say the least. I've learned so much about myself, about people, and about what it takes to be a good leader. Here are some of the things I've learned along the way: 
- NOT saying No is the hardest part for me and I think I paid a heavy price for it in the first 6 months.
- Establishing Culture isn't easy and you can't do it in a day or week or month and definitely not when you're also doing Architecture + Development + DevOps. It takes time and consistency. and it cannot be done just by saying it. you've to do it again and again and again
- The case of your best engineer leaving you (switching jobs), just before the most critical time for the company, and you can't do anything about it. You could have let go 2 other engineers to match the payscale he was going for but then is it worth it? My philosphy is if someone has made up their mind and have gotten an offer from somewhere, even if you match it now, he'll probably go 6 months down the road. so, if you value him so much, then don't let him even come to the stage of starting to think of it. But then somethings are not in your control too, especially when you're working in a smaller company/startup. 
- Have recently rolled out the 360 degree feebacks mechanism in the company and trying to tie those to the appraisal cycle, they didn't have any process before. The idea is to not decide someone's appraisal based on vibes and mood of the senior only. I've experienced that in my previous company and found that to be pretty good.
- standardizing 1-1s and feedbacks for the team, especially when you're new to management and have a team that's also new to you. My 1-1s were not structured, they were just random conversations, and same was the case with feedbacks. I often recommend my team members to read "Managing Oneself" by Peter Druker and try to assess their strengths. 
- One challenge I am facing currently is that sometimes people dont take feedback nicely and it's becoming hard for me to make them understand that it's in their favour. Trying to get better at it.
- One thing, I think I've done reasonably well and do take pride in, as a new manager, is keeping the team motivated and their morale up even though they were working overtime and under pressure for a significant amount of time, as we're short on time and under-staffed, for this release. I am now discussing the ways to compensate the team with higher ups, let's see how it goes.
- Process level thinking. instilling the culture of ownership. I feel that we are fortunate to be in the industry where we have the luxury to work with smart people so if you just instill the ownership in them, they can do wonders for you. And I've seen that happening in the past year or so, they are stepping up and made this big release happen.


### Part 2: (Apr, 2025)

- Continuing where we left off, I was able to negotiate extra PTOs (1 week for each team member) for the team as a compensation for working overtime and under pressure during the release. Given that the team was happy with it, I'd take that as a win for now. 
- I was trying to structure my 1-1s (following the styles recommended in the book 'The Manager's Path') with the team but then I left that effort and now of the opinion that those are better structured in the unstructured manner, at least for me, because my goals are: 1. I am able to connect with them on a human level, 2. that people should be able to come up and talk about what they want to talk about and 3. at the same time, I am able to get across what I want to get across. Maybe the only structure there should be is to have a 1-1 with each team member on a bi-weekly basis (thinking of doing monthly with some). What I do want to cover in my unstructured 1-1s is the following: C
  - General wellbeing on a personal level: things like: How are you doing? How are you feeling? Are you able to maintain a work life balance? Ask about family, interests, hobbies, etc (I keep a log of people's interest and come up with something to talk about once in a while)
  - Feedbacks: they share theirs first regarding me, company and the team, then I share mine.
  - Career development: Are you learning new things? Are you growing in your career? Are you happy with your current role? Is there anything you'd like to learn or do differently? Anything I can help them with their career development.
- I am also improving the structure of 360 degree feebacks, by trying to make it more structured and meaningful and less about just filling forms. The idea is to get actionable insights from the feedbacks and be able to act on them. I've realized that people generally don't share much unless specifically asked via specific questions. I've tried to make the questions more specific and actionable. 
- A couple of big challenges I am currently facing are: 
  1. managing the type of Engineers who "think" they are very capable and technically strong but are not at that level, not yet probably. they would portray themselves as always overdoing things and taking on too much work which leads to them under-delivering on the things they committed to. 
  2. Managing the Alpha Geeks (as per Camille Fournier's "The Manager's Path"). they really give you tough time. I've tried everything from direct feedback to making them realize that their behavior is not good for the team, but it seems to be not working so far
- improving productivity and quality.


### Part 3: (Oct, 2025)

- Well, for starter, I just couldn't work with those 2 types in my team and had to ask the management to replace them. It wasn't working for both of us, they were stressed and the team morale was taking a hit, and so was mine.
- The team culture is getting better and better day by day. People are now more happy and open to discussions and sharing their thoughts. The sense of ownership is also growing and people are taking more responsibility. However, the quality is still a bit of a concern, with bugs spilling here and there. We're working on it.
- The 360 degree feedback mechanism is now more structured and meaningful. People are now more open to sharing their thoughts and giving (and taking as well) feedbacks.
- Lately, we have worked on streamlining the onboarding and the new joiners (we're growing :P) now get onboarded quickly and are able to contribute quickly. The idea is to get them productive within their first couple of days (and not like weeks, as was the case previously)
- Delegating effectively, I've just started working on this with my team members and new leads. I myself had this habbit of doing everything, until recently, on my own (especially when it's about development) because I felt I can do it better/quickly on my own.
- While, there's some sense of ownership in the team, I also want to inculcate accountability in the team. I think that will also fix those quality concerns and bugs spilling away.


### Part 4: (Draft: Aug, 2026)

- AI is changing the dynamics of everything. People think that with AI, the engineering management would become easier. I don't know if I agree with that, because what I've experienced is that in addition to humans, now you also have to manage Agents and they do give you tough time.
